# Wireframe feedback questionnaire - Phase 2

Used to gather review feedback on wireframes v1 of *The Internet in Brazil,
1995-2026*. The completed responses and the resulting design changes are the
evidence for Phase 2 of the coursework.

## How many reviewers, and who

Target: **four to five reviewers.** Nielsen Norman Group's finding that five
users uncover roughly 85% of usability problems, with sharply diminishing
returns after that, is the basis for the number rather than convenience
(Nielsen, J., 2000. *Why You Only Need to Test with 5 Users*. Nielsen Norman
Group).

Aim for a spread rather than five of the same person:

- at least one Android and one iPhone user, since the brief is specifically
  about responsive layouts
- at least one non-technical reviewer, who judges the site as a reader rather
  than as a developer
- ideally one person who was online in Brazil during the 1995-2005 period, who
  is the actual target reader and will notice what is missing

## Running the session

Send the reviewer the wireframe board and ask them to open at least one page on
their own phone. Then ask the eight questions below.

Say this first, or some version of it:

> These are rough layout sketches, not the finished design - there is no colour
> and the grey bars are placeholder text. I am trying to find out what is
> confusing or missing, so please be blunt. "It looks nice" does not help me.

That framing matters. Reviewers default to politeness, and polite feedback
cannot evidence a design change.

## The questions

1. **Five-second test.** Look at the Home page for five seconds, then look
   away. What is this site about, and who do you think it is for?

2. **Navigation labels.** Without clicking anything: what do you expect to find
   behind each menu item?

3. **Directed task.** You want to find out when broadband arrived in Brazil.
   Where would you click first?

4. **Visual hierarchy.** On the Home page, what does your eye land on first?
   What second?

5. **Phone layout.** On your phone, is anything hard to read or awkward to
   reach with your thumb? Would you keep scrolling to the bottom of the page?

6. **Missing and surplus.** What did you expect to see that is not there? Is
   anything on the page that seems unnecessary?

7. **Desktop against phone.** Comparing the two widths, does anything get lost
   or become harder to follow on the smaller screen?

8. **One change.** If you could change one single thing, what would it be?

## What to record for each reviewer

Copy the block below once per reviewer. Verbatim quotes matter more than
paraphrase: a direct quote evidences the criticism, a summary reads as
something the author could have invented.

```
Reviewer:        (first name or initials)
Relationship:    (classmate, colleague, family, friend)
Device used:     (e.g. Android phone 6.1", MacBook 13")
Date:
Format:          (WhatsApp, in person, call)

Q1 first impression:
Q2 nav expectations:
Q3 where they clicked:
Q4 eye path:
Q5 phone issues:
Q6 missing / surplus:
Q7 desktop vs phone:
Q8 one change:

Direct quotes worth keeping:
-

Action taken:
- [ ] changed the design - describe what and where
- [ ] not changed - record why, since a reasoned rejection is also a valid
      response to feedback and worth reporting
```

## Privacy

Record first names or initials only. If a screenshot of a conversation is used
as evidence in the report, obscure phone numbers, profile photographs and
surnames, and ask the person whether they mind being quoted.

## After the responses arrive

1. Collate every issue raised into one list, noting how many reviewers hit the
   same thing. Anything raised independently by two or more people is a real
   problem, not a preference.
2. Decide what changes, and record the reasoning for anything deliberately left
   alone.
3. Produce wireframes v2 and a change log pairing each change to the feedback
   that prompted it.
4. Section C of the report draws directly on that change log.
