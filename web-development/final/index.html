The Internet in Brazil, 1995-2026

In 1995 the Brazilian internet left the universities and was opened to the public. Thirty years later the country runs one of the world's largest instant payment systems on it. This site follows what happened in between.

Why Brazil

Brazil is a useful country to study because it did not simply import the internet. It built its own governance model in 1995, through a multi-stakeholder committee rather than a ministry. It produced the social network that beat Facebook at home for six years. It wrote a civil rights framework for the internet in 2014 that was copied and cited internationally. And in 2020 its central bank launched a payment system that reached more people in three years than most private wallets manage in a decade.

It is also a country where access arrived late and unevenly, and where the way people connect, through a shared computer in a neighbourhood shop, then a prepaid phone, is not the way the story is usually told in Europe or North America.
